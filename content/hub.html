MarGine User Guide

Marketing and Growth Command Center

MarGine is TaxAce's Marketing Engine. It helps the TaxAce team plan, write, improve, and check marketing content: social posts, carousels, posters, reels, blog articles, emails, YouTube scripts, brochures, flyers, and replies to client and lead messages.

MarGine is not a general-purpose content generator. It coordinates a team of specialist Skills so each request follows the right process, and it uses the TaxAce Organizational Intelligence Dossier as its source of truth.

How MarGine Works

Every request moves through the same four steps:

  1. Understand. MarGine reads your request and works out what you are asking for.
  2. Route. It checks its Team Roster and picks the right specialist Skill, or a sequence of Skills if the job needs more than one.
  3. Create. The specialist does the work using its own process.
  4. Check. Every deliverable is reviewed for brand voice, TaxAce rules, call-to-action placement, and tax accuracy before you see it.

The checks run in the background. You will not see a list of what passed or what was fixed. You will see the finished work, or a short note if something needs your decision.

The routing line. Every response starts with a line showing who is handling the request and why, for example: Route: margine-content-engine Why: new social post. If you are refining something MarGine just wrote, you will see Continuing: followed by the Skill name instead.

MarGine Asks Before It Produces

MarGine does not guess. If something that would change the result is missing, it stops and asks before writing anything. There is no rough draft first and no list of assumptions afterward.

When it asks, it sends one compact message with all its questions together, and each question comes with a suggested answer. You can reply in a word ("yes," "the first one") to accept the suggestions, or change any of them.

MarGine may ask about:

  • The source or topic
  • The platform
  • The content type
  • The purpose of the piece
  • The audience
  • Length or runtime
  • Which option you chose or which version to revise
  • Any rate, threshold, deadline, form, or state it cannot confirm from a source you provided

It will not re-ask anything you have already told it in the conversation.

Tip: The more you include in your first message, the fewer questions you will get.

Creating Social Content

For any social request, MarGine confirms four things, unless you have already stated them:

Question Examples
Platform Facebook and Instagram (4:5 vertical) is the suggested default, or name another platform
Content type Static poster, carousel, text-only post, or reel
Purpose Tax tip, general knowledge, TaxAce insight, motivational post, meme, promoting TaxAce services, or your own
Runtime (reels only) A suggested length is offered

The purpose changes the angle of the piece. It never changes TaxAce's standards.

What you receive

  • Every post includes a caption ending in three call-to-action options, tied to that piece's angle. The caption's CTA never contains a link or phone number. The real link, phone, or email goes in a separate first-comment block.
  • Static posters also include three Hook/Header + Subheader options. Headers are written in Title Case.
  • Reels include the full copy package: script (built to your runtime), in-video text, caption, and three thumbnail copy options.
  • Carousels and text-only posts do not include a poster or thumbnail block.

Choose one option (or ask to blend two). Your selected pair is used word for word on the poster or thumbnail.

Turning one piece into another

You can ask MarGine to reshape an approved piece, for example "turn this into a carousel." Every decision already made carries over, including a deliberate "no CTA" on a public warning post, hedged wording, and any flag about a claim. Platform and purpose carry over unless you say otherwise.

Optional: images in Gamma and Canva

When Gamma is connected, MarGine asks one question after you approve a social piece: whether you want to push it to Gamma. Nothing happens unless you say yes.

  1. On a yes, MarGine gives you five poster ideas.
  2. You pick one, and MarGine builds the final prompt and generates the image in Gamma. You never write a prompt yourself.
  3. You approve the image or ask for changes.
  4. Only after you approve, MarGine asks whether you want to edit it in Canva.

On a Canva-template poster, the top band is left empty for the TaxAce header. The header is fixed template artwork and is added by a person in Canva. It is never AI-generated. MarGine does not use Canva to create or auto-fill posters.

What MarGine Will and Will Not Do

TaxAce rules MarGine always follows:

  • Tax Strategy is never renamed "tax planning." If approved source material itself uses that wording, MarGine will copy it faithfully and flag the conflict in the same response.
  • No guaranteed savings, refunds, or IRS outcomes.
  • No pricing unless you ask for it.
  • No client names, results, or staff names unless you ask for them or the deliverable requires them.
  • No em dashes in marketing copy.

Tax facts:

  • Dates and deadlines are checked against the TaxAce Current Tax Facts document (2026 version).
  • Rates, thresholds, and dollar figures are trusted when they come from a source you provided, such as an IRS.gov article or a TaxAce-published piece. A number that does not trace back to a source you supplied is never filled in from memory. MarGine will ask you to confirm it or provide the source.
  • State-specific deadlines in the reference document are California only. For other states, MarGine will ask rather than assume.

If something is held: you will see one plain sentence explaining what needs your decision, with a suggested answer.

To see the checks: ask "show the gate report" or "what did you check?" and MarGine will give you the full report.

What MarGine Does Not Cover

Request Where it goes
Newsletter issues, subscriber growth, newsletter metrics Newella
Website architecture, page copy, site conversion Webtrix
Internal SOPs Not built yet. MarGine will tell you plainly and ask how you want to proceed

If MarGine cannot do something, it says so instead of improvising a substitute.

How to Get the Best Results

A strong first message includes:

  • What you want to accomplish
  • Who the content is for
  • What deliverable you need
  • Where it will be published or used
  • What action the audience should take
  • Any source article, facts, deadlines, or restrictions

You do not need to name a Skill. Describe the outcome and MarGine will choose the route.

Always include your source. For anything involving a tax rule, rate, or deadline, paste the IRS.gov article, TaxAce piece, or other verified source you want the content based on. This is the fastest way to get a piece that clears without questions.

Five Best Prompts

1. Write a Social Post From a Source

Write a [static poster / carousel / text post / reel] for [Facebook and Instagram] based on the article below. The purpose is [tax tip / TaxAce insight / etc.]. Keep the claims faithful to the source. [Paste article or link]

2. Build a Marketing Campaign

Create a campaign for [service or offer]. The audience is [audience], and the goal is [desired action]. Develop five distinct campaign angles, recommend the strongest, and write the landing-page copy, launch email, and social post.

3. Turn a Tax Topic Into Long-Form Content

Using the source below, explain what [tax topic or update] means for [audience]. Create a blog article and a voice-only YouTube script. Keep federal and state rules distinct where relevant. [Paste source]

4. Improve an Existing Asset

Improve the copy below for clarity and conversion. The reader should [desired action]. Keep the TaxAce voice calm, confident, and practical. Tell me the main weaknesses first, then give the revised version. [Paste copy]

5. Reply to a Message

Draft a reply to this [DM / comment / email]. It is from a [lead / existing client]. The goal is [goal]. [Paste message]

Working With Follow-Ups

  • Picking an option: reply with the number, such as "Option 2," or "Option 2 with the subheader from Option 1."
  • Revising: say what to change. If your reference could mean more than one thing ("make it shorter" when two pieces exist), MarGine will ask which one you mean.
  • Changes that affect a claim: MarGine confirms with you before changing the meaning of a tax statement.
  • Approving suggestions: "Yes" to a set of suggested answers is enough. MarGine will not ask again.

Quick Prompt Check

Before sending, ask yourself:

  • Is the result I want clear?
  • Did I name the audience and the platform or destination?
  • Did I include my source for any tax claim?
  • Did I say what action the audience should take?
  • Did I mention deadlines or restrictions?

If most of these are covered, MarGine can usually begin right away with few or no questions.

What Is Newella?

Newella is TaxAce's monthly newsletter writer and editor. It takes the newsletter from the TaxAce calendar to a finished, GHL-ready issue: deadlines, topics, copy, subject lines, hero banner, infographic posters, and the Client Highlight.

Newella is designed for:

  • Recommending the deadlines and topics that belong in each month's issue
  • Drafting the full five-section newsletter
  • Writing three equal options for subject lines, preview texts, and hero hook/header and subheader
  • Writing the one closing call to action (a booked Discovery Call)
  • Creating the hero banner, infographic posters, and Client Highlight quote poster in Gamma
  • Checking every date, claim, and word before you approve

Newella does one job: the monthly newsletter. Growth plans, lead magnets, welcome sequences, newsletter metrics, social posts, blogs, and video scripts are handled by MarGine or a separate project.

How Newella Works

Newella recommends first, drafts second. It will not write the newsletter until you confirm the details.

Step What Newella does What you do
1. Recommend Reads the TaxAce Recurring Master Calendar, verifies the dates, and recommends the month's deadlines plus 3 to 5 Tax Topic and 3 to 5 Business Tax Tip candidates Say: "Let's create the [Month] newsletter."
2. Approve Records your decisions and asks about anything complicated or unclear Approve the deadlines. Pick 2 Tax Topics and 2 Business Tax Tips.
3. Client Highlight Asks for the client details (only after step 2 is approved) Provide the client, approval, contact line, and quote
4. Confirm Shows an Intake Summary Card Correct anything, then reply "Intake confirmed."
5. Draft Writes the issue in a Claude Doc from the Issue Template Review
6. Options Offers 3 subject lines, 3 preview texts, 3 hero hook/header and subheader sets, and the one CTA Pick one of each
7. Final check Checks every line and fixes mechanical problems Review anything Newella flags
8. Approve copy Sets the status to Copy approved Approve the copy
9. Visuals Creates each image in Gamma, one at a time Say "generate visuals." Review each image before the next.
10. Hand off Prepares the GHL-ready version, file names, and alt text Upload and place the images in GHL, then send

What You Receive

  • One Claude Doc titled TaxAce [Month Year] Client Newsletter
  • Five sections: What's Inside, Important Deadlines, Tax Topics, Business Tax Tips, and Client Highlights, ending with one CTA
  • A verification table that records the source for every date, number, and claim
  • Six visuals: a 1920 x 1080 hero banner, four infographic posters (two Tax Topics, two Business Tax Tips), and a Client Highlight quote poster
  • A clean, paste-ready version for GHL

How Deadlines Are Chosen

Newella reads the TaxAce Recurring Master Calendar. It follows these rules:

  • The newsletter releases on the 1st of every month.
  • Deadlines appear in the issue for the month they are actually due. If a date lands on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day.
  • Items marked for advance notice also appear one month early, with their real due date. For example, the November issue lists the December 22 sales tax prepayment.
  • Sales tax prepayments use TaxAce's client date on the 22nd.
  • Only client-facing deadlines are included. Holidays, birthdays, work anniversaries, and team tasks are never included.
  • Anything complicated or unclear is a question for you, not a guess.

How to Get the Best Results

You do not need to name a specialist or a tool. Describe where you are in the month, and Newella continues from there. Helpful inputs include:

  • The issue month
  • Any topics you want included or avoided
  • For the Client Highlight: the client's name, their written approval, the source document, the approved contact line, and the exact quote or message
  • The attribution you want on the quote (the owner's name or the business name)
  • The Discovery Call booking link, if Newella asks for it

Never ask Newella to guess a number, a date, or a link. If it cannot verify something, it will tag it UNVERIFIED and ask you.

Five Best Prompts

1. Start the Month

Let's create the [Month] newsletter.

Best for: Getting Newella's deadline and topic recommendations. It will not draft or ask a long list of setup questions first.

2. Approve Deadlines and Choose Topics

Approved, with these changes: [drop or add items]. Tax Topics: [numbers or names]. Business Tax Tips: [numbers or names]. For [Ask-first item], [answer].

Best for: Locking the issue's content so drafting starts from confirmed facts.

3. Provide the Client Highlight

Feature [client business name]. Written approval: [who approved, date]. Source document: [attached]. Approved contact line: [phone, email, website, license]. Quote: "[exact quote]". Attribute it to [owner name / business name].

Best for: Giving Newella everything for the Client Highlight and the quote poster in one message.

4. Confirm and Choose Options

Intake confirmed.

Then, after the draft:

Use subject 2, preview 1, and hero option 3.

Best for: Starting the draft, then choosing the inbox packaging and hero text.

5. Create the Visuals

Copy approved. Generate visuals. Start with the hero banner.

Best for: Creating images one at a time. Newella confirms the plan for each image, generates it in Gamma, shows it, and waits for your approval before starting the next.

Visuals

  • Images are created only after the copy is approved.
  • Every word on an image comes from the approved copy. If you want a wording change, change it before the image is generated.
  • Every image uses the TaxAce #00aa91 theme color. Body text is black. Boxes that hold text are dark teal (#02544a) with white text.
  • The hero banner is exactly 1920 x 1080 px. Posters can be portrait or landscape, whichever fits the content.
  • Each image should be 2 MB or less. JPG is preferred.
  • Gamma cannot edit an image it already made, and each generation is billed. If you want a change, Newella will tell you whether it needs a quick fix, a regeneration, or a Canva polish.
  • Canva (TaxAce Social Media kit) is an optional polish step.
  • Client photos are never created or altered by AI. You add them yourself.
  • Newella cannot download the images. Download them from Gamma promptly, because export links expire after about a week. Then upload the final images to the chat for Newella's image check.

Important Guardrails

  • Newella recommends before it drafts, and does not draft until you reply "Intake confirmed."
  • The Client Highlight is requested only after the deadlines and topics are approved.
  • Each issue has two Tax Topics and two Business Tax Tips.
  • Subject lines, preview texts, and hero options come as three equal choices, never ranked.
  • The issue ends with exactly one CTA to book a Discovery Call. No individual is named in it.
  • Every date, rate, threshold, form, and tax claim is verified, supplied by TaxAce, or tagged UNVERIFIED. An issue with unverified items stays in Draft.
  • Tax Strategy is never called tax planning.
  • Pricing, client results, and staff names stay hidden. The Client Highlight is the exception, and only with written client approval.
  • Newella never promises savings, refunds, or IRS outcomes.
  • Newella cannot open, send, schedule, or change anything in GHL, and will never say it did. If you edit the issue in GHL, paste those edits back into the Claude Doc before asking for revisions.

Status Labels

Every delivery ends with one status line:

  • Draft: a check failed, a fact is unverified, or an item is open
  • Ready for your review: all checks passed and every fact is verified or supplied by TaxAce
  • Copy approved: you approved the copy and visuals may begin
  • Visuals in progress: at least one image is being created, reviewed, or polished
  • Ready for GHL: all copy is approved and all six visuals passed the image check

Making Changes

Newella changes only what you ask for and shows only the changed part. Examples:

Change the October 15 line to say [wording].

Swap Tax Topic 2 for the third candidate.

Make the Client Highlight shorter.

If a change affects copy you already approved, Newella will say so and move the status back to Draft.

A Simple Way to Start

Let's create the [Month] newsletter.

Newella will take it from there, one step at a time, and ask for your approval before each major stage.

What Is Vidora?

Vidora turns an idea, or content you already have, into everything you need to produce a short-form reel or video with AI tools. It focuses on video and reel content only.

Vidora can:

  • Develop a reel or video concept from scratch.
  • Write or fit your copy to an exact runtime.
  • Audit your existing script for word count, pacing, claims, call to action, and brand fit.
  • Build a written, timed cinematic storyboard.
  • Write a storyboard image prompt you can paste into any text-to-image tool.
  • Write ready-to-use video-generation prompts for HeyGen, Higgsfield, CapCut, or any comparable tool.
  • Advise on HeyGen apps, CapCut workflows, and other generator modes, and help you repair a result that came out wrong.

Vidora does not render, edit, publish, or upload videos or images. It writes the plans and prompts. You paste them into your generator and bring the result back for review. Vidora will never say something was created unless a connected tool actually completed it.

Vidora stays within video and reel content. If you ask for something else, it will tell you it is outside its scope and suggest where to take it. If a video needs a supporting asset such as a poster or thumbnail, Vidora will name the tool it would use, explain why, and ask your permission first. A "yes" covers that one use only.

How Vidora Works

Vidora follows four stages. You approve each one before it moves on.

Stage What you get Your options at the checkpoint
1. Copy Creative Lock and timed, runtime-fitted reel copy Approve and continue, approve and stop, or revise
2. Written cinematic storyboard Scene-by-scene timed plan Approve, revise, or stop
3. Storyboard image prompt (optional) One paste-ready prompt for a storyboard sheet Say yes or no when asked
4. Final generator-ready prompt A prompt package for your chosen tool Review and use

A question, a comment, or silence is not approval. Vidora only moves forward when you clearly approve. If you ask for changes, it revises the current stage and stops at the same checkpoint.

Two ways to begin

A. Start from scratch. Type Start, or describe your idea. Vidora checks what it already knows, then asks only for what is missing.

B. Bring your own content. Paste your script, outline, or narration. Vidora keeps your intent, audits it, and fits it to your runtime. Tell it whether your wording must stay exact or can be optimized.

You can also jump in at any stage. Paste finished copy to go straight to the storyboard, paste a written storyboard to go to prompting, or ask for a final generator prompt directly. Vidora will ask only for the material it still needs.

Duration is always required

Every reel needs an explicit target length (for example, 15 seconds, 30 seconds, or 45 seconds) before Vidora finalizes copy, pacing, or scenes. Vidora will not guess it from the platform or apply a default. Once you approve a runtime, it is locked until you change it.

If your script is too long for the runtime, Vidora will tell you and offer a faithful shorter version or a longer runtime. It will not quietly cut or rush your content.

Stage 3: The Optional Storyboard Image Prompt

After you approve the written storyboard, Vidora asks once whether you want a storyboard image prompt. You can say no and go straight to the final prompt.

If you say yes:

  • Vidora asks, in one message, only what is unsettled: reference images that must be followed, a visual style other than the default (ultra-photorealistic), and whether you also want clean frame prompts for each cut.
  • It writes one tool-neutral prompt for an annotated storyboard sheet. One sheet covers up to six cuts, and longer reels use two sheets.
  • The prompt starts with a text list of every word that should appear on the sheet, and keeps captions short, so the image tool does not invent text. It does not specify size or aspect ratio.
  • You paste the prompt into the text-to-image tool of your choice.
  • Upload the image it generates, and Vidora will check it against the word list and the written storyboard, tell you what matches and what is wrong, and rewrite only the failing parts. This check allows up to two correction rounds.

Note that a prompt is not an image. Vidora can only treat an image as real if you upload it.

Gamma is not part of the normal workflow. If you want a Gamma storyboard, ask for it. Vidora will ask for a separate explicit yes right before using it. If you request changes, the whole Gamma project is re-created.

Stage 4: The Generator Intake

Before writing the final prompt, Vidora asks a few short questions so the prompt fits your tool. It asks only about what is not already settled.

1. Which tool, app, or workflow? Vidora asks this first.

  • HeyGen: Creative Studio, Cinematic Shots, Video Podcast, Video Agent, AI Studio, PPT/PDF to Video, Batch Mode, Interactive Video, LiveAvatar, Translate Videos, AI Clipping, Upscale Video, or Speech Cleanup.
  • CapCut: the AI video maker, an image-to-video draft, or editing and finishing in the CapCut editor.
  • Higgsfield or another generator: Vidora asks which mode you are using. If it has no documented guidance for a mode, it will ask you to read out the fields you see on screen and will never invent controls, flags, or model names.

Apps that start from an existing video or document (such as Translate Videos, AI Clipping, Upscale Video, or PPT/PDF to Video) need your source file and the outcome you want instead of an avatar or speech mode.

2. Reference. Will you use a saved avatar or character in the tool, a storyboard you generated on another platform, or both?

3. Speech mode. Choose one:

  • Speaking script: the subject talks on camera.
  • Pure narration: a voice-over, with the subject silent.
  • A combination of both.
  • If the video is visual-only, tell Vidora and it will skip this.

4. Other items that affect the result. For example voice, language, music and sound, and whether captions are made in the tool or added later.

Vidora asks question 1 first. Once it knows your tool, it asks questions 2 to 4 together in one message.

The Cinematic Standard

Every video prompt Vidora writes aims for a cinematic, movie-style result, not a moving picture.

  • Subjects perform realistic actions with believable weight, timing, gesture, and gaze.
  • Every cut shows a visible change from start to end.
  • Each cut has a main action, a secondary action, a varied camera move, and something moving in the environment.
  • No long holds, and no ending on a frozen pose.
  • Every final frame resolves the setup or call to action.

A static image or slideshow is never the intended result.

If your result looks like a slideshow

Tell Vidora what you used and what you saw. Common fixes:

  • Check that you picked the right app for the job. Some apps animate uploaded images lightly, while others are built for motion. Tell Vidora which one you used.
  • Turn on the tool's cinematic or animated-visuals option if it has one, and say which model or mode you selected.
  • Upload clean individual frames, not a storyboard sheet with captions and panels. A composite sheet alone is not enough for image-to-video.
  • Ask Vidora to rewrite the prompt with stronger motion in each cut.

How to Get the Best Results

Include as many of these as you know:

  • The goal of the reel (awareness, education, lead generation, trust-building).
  • The audience, if it is not obvious.
  • The TaxAce service or topic.
  • The platform and where it will be published.
  • The exact duration.
  • The format: avatar on camera, voiceover, dialogue, or visual-only.
  • Orientation (vertical, horizontal, or square).
  • The call to action or ending you want.
  • Facts, brand details, characters, or locations that must appear.
  • Reference assets: avatars, photos, brand visuals, or earlier approved storyboards.
  • Whether your supplied wording must stay exact.

If you are unsure about anything, just say so. Vidora will ask for what it needs.

Five Best Prompts

1. Start from scratch

Start

Vidora will guide you from there.

2. Use your own script, with exact wording

I have a finished script for a 30-second Instagram Reel. Keep the wording exactly as written. Check whether it fits the runtime and flag anything that needs fixing. Here is the script: [paste script]

3. Create the storyboard image prompt

The written storyboard is approved. Yes, I would like a storyboard image prompt. Use the default ultra-photorealistic style and include clean frame prompts for each cut.

4. Create the final prompt for a specific tool

The storyboard is approved. I will use HeyGen Creative Studio with a saved avatar. The avatar should speak on camera for the whole video. Write the final prompt.

5. Repair a static result

I used HeyGen Video Agent with the storyboard sheet attached and the result looked like a slideshow. Here is what I saw: [describe]. Rewrite the prompt so every cut has real subject movement and camera motion, and tell me what to change in the tool.

Important Notes

  • Service terminology. Vidora uses TaxAce's own terms. "Tax Strategy" stays "Tax Strategy" and is not renamed "tax planning."
  • Verified claims only. Vidora verifies current tax, legal, regulatory, deadline, rate, threshold, form, and state-specific claims against official sources before using them in copy, narration, on-screen text, or prompts. A disclaimer does not make an unverified claim acceptable.
  • No fear-based marketing. No guarantees, invented savings, or unsupported outcomes.
  • Hidden by default. Pricing, testimonials, individual names, and credential-holder identities stay out of the content unless you explicitly ask for them and TaxAce's approved source material supports them.
  • No invented assets. Vidora does not invent logos, government seals, forms, letters, testimonials, savings figures, prices, legal text, credentials, or identifiable people.
  • Marketing copy style. TaxAce marketing copy is written without em dashes.
  • Tool details change. Platforms update their apps, models, and durations often. Vidora checks current guidance where it can. Where a tool is not documented, it will ask you to read out the fields you see and will not guess.
  • Capability honesty. Vidora plans and writes. You generate, edit, and publish in your own tools.

Marketing landing pages and websites, built as ready-to-use HTML

Webtrix is TaxAce's dedicated web builder. It works like a senior web designer, developer, and conversion copywriter in one. You tell it what you want to achieve, and it plans the project with you, studies what strong work looks like in your category, writes the copy, designs an original interactive experience, generates the imagery, and hands you finished HTML files.

The files are ready to paste into GoHighLevel (GHL) or deploy on GitHub, Vercel, or any other host.

What Webtrix Can Do

  • Build a landing page: one HTML file with sections and buttons that guide a visitor through a single conversion journey.
  • Build a multi-page website: separate, linked HTML pages (for example Home, Services, About, Contact). Never one page that fakes pages with tabs.
  • Review a plan, concept, brief, or requirements document, tell you what to improve, and build from it.
  • Redesign or rewrite an existing page you share with it, as HTML.
  • Write hook-first marketing copy with SEO, GEO, and AEO built in (see the glossary below).
  • Design with real depth: scroll effects, parallax, 3D elements, motion, and interactive details, each chosen for a reason.
  • Generate ultra-photorealistic hero, banner, section, and background images with Gamma when you do not supply your own.
  • Adapt to other web projects, such as a calculator, a simple tool, or an internal page, when static HTML can honestly deliver it.

Webtrix works on TaxAce projects and outside client projects. For TaxAce work it uses the TaxAce Organizational Intelligence Dossier as the source of truth for the business, audience, services, positioning, voice, and professional boundaries. For an outside project it does not use TaxAce's information at all. It learns the business from you.

How Webtrix Works

There are five phases and one approval gate.

1. Planning

Planning always comes first. Webtrix opens by asking whether you already have a plan, concept, idea, or planning document.

  • If you have something: share it, including an uploaded document or blueprint. Webtrix reviews it, tells you what to keep, change, and add, and asks only about what is still unresolved.
  • If you have nothing: Webtrix asks a short set of questions, usually no more than six or seven, all in one turn. Each question comes with Webtrix's recommendation, so answering "yes" is a valid reply.

2. Research

Before designing, Webtrix looks for three to five relevant, high-quality reference sites for direction. It tells you what it is borrowing from each and what it is deliberately not copying. The design itself is original.

If live research is not available, Webtrix uses its own stored knowledge of strong work in your category, so you do not have to do the research. It will tell you plainly that the references come from its knowledge and not live research, and that sites may have changed since. If you have sites you like, send them. Yours always take priority.

3. One build plan, one approval

Webtrix presents a short plan: the pages or sections, the headline hook, the conversion path, the look and feel, the image plan, the references, and the SEO approach. You then approve it, or ask for changes. Webtrix does not start building until you approve.

4. Build

Webtrix writes the copy, designs the experience, generates the images, and writes the code in one pass.

5. Verify and deliver

Webtrix checks the files with an automated checker and gives you the finished files plus short notes on how to use them. After delivery, a revision is simply "change this."

What Webtrix Asks You, and What It Decides

Webtrix keeps questions short by deciding the design details itself.

Webtrix may ask you:

  • Whether the project is for TaxAce or an outside client
  • Landing page, website, or something else
  • The one outcome that counts, and where the main button leads (a booking link, a form, a phone number)
  • The offer and who it is for (outside projects only; TaxAce projects already have this in the Dossier)
  • What you already have: copy, logo, brand colors, photos, and sites you like
  • Where it will live: GHL, GitHub, Vercel, or another host, and which pages a website needs
  • Functionality, only when the project needs it (a form, calculator, or booking)
  • Anything the page will state as fact that Webtrix cannot verify

Webtrix decides for you, and shows its choices in the plan:

  • Colors, fonts, layout, and section order
  • How much motion, depth, and 3D to use
  • Image direction
  • Headline options and the hook angle
  • Keywords, structured data, FAQ topics, and page names

If you supply a brand asset or a reference, it overrides Webtrix's choice.

What You Receive

  • A landing page: one self-contained .html file.
  • A website: separate linked .html files, plus sitemap.xml and robots.txt for hosts that use them.
  • Delivery notes: what is in each file, the values you need to confirm, how to paste or deploy for your host, a link map for websites, what was checked, and any assumptions.

Each file has a settings block at the top, called CONFIG. It holds the places you may need to edit, such as the main button destination, contact details, image links, and page links. One edit there updates the whole file.

Using the Files on Your Host

Webtrix has not tested files inside GHL, so treat these as typical behavior and confirm on your first paste or deploy.

  • GHL: paste the page into a custom code or custom HTML element. Some GHL elements remove the page wrapper tags. If that happens, Webtrix's notes show where to move the page title, meta tags, and structured data. For a website, each page goes into its own GHL page.
  • GitHub: commit the files, and index.html is the home page.
  • Vercel: deploy the files directly. Webtrix can include an optional settings file for security headers.

About Images

When you do not supply images, Webtrix generates them with Gamma.

  • Every image is ultra-photorealistic and creative. Webtrix does not use cartoon, illustration, or stylized images.
  • Images contain no text, logos, or recognizable brands, and no made-up clients, team members, or testimonials.
  • Webtrix tells you how many images it plans to generate before building, because each generation is billed.
  • Gamma provides hosted links to the images. Webtrix cannot confirm those links will stay live or load inside GHL, so it recommends that you save each image and upload it to your own host, then replace the link in the file's CONFIG block.
  • If Gamma is unavailable, Webtrix says so, builds with intentional color and gradient fallbacks, and gives you the image prompts to run yourself.

Forms and Data

A standalone HTML file cannot receive form submissions by itself, and Webtrix will never build a form that only pretends to submit. Instead it uses one of these, in order of preference:

  1. Your own GHL form or calendar embed
  2. A booking or contact link as the main button
  3. A form that sends to an endpoint or webhook that you provide
  4. A phone or email link, labeled honestly

TaxAce Projects: What Webtrix Protects

For TaxAce work, Webtrix applies the Dossier automatically.

  • Tax Strategy is presented as TaxAce's primary proactive service and is never renamed "tax planning."
  • Copy follows the TaxAce voice: premium, calm, confident, warm, strategic, and clear. It avoids fear-based tax marketing, banned phrases, and em dashes.
  • Pricing, client proof, testimonials, and individual names stay hidden unless you ask for them and they are approved. When pricing is used, it keeps the exact approved amounts and qualifiers like "starts at." A real client result is treated as evidence for that client, never as a promise.
  • Webtrix does not guarantee tax savings, refunds, IRS outcomes, or eligibility.
  • Current tax facts are verified before they are stated. If a fact cannot be verified, Webtrix leaves it out or asks you to supply it with its source.
  • It writes for TaxAce's audience without announcing a revenue threshold.

Boundaries

  • Webtrix produces files. It does not deploy, publish, change DNS, or edit a live website.
  • It does not build a server-side application. If a project needs accounts, a database, or payments, Webtrix plans it, builds what static HTML can honestly do, and recommends a stack for the rest.
  • It does not write prompts for other AI website builders, and it does not run formal website audit reports. Webtrix builds the files itself, and it can review and improve a plan or existing page that you share.
  • It does not invent results, testimonials, ratings, prices, credentials, statistics, links, offers, or deadlines.
  • It will not claim to have tested a page in a browser, on a phone, or inside GHL unless that actually happened. It tells you exactly what was checked and what was not. The checker covers the code, such as links, markup, structured data, and accessibility basics. Look at the finished page in your browser and on your phone before launching.
  • For work that is not a web build, such as social posts, newsletters, or video, Webtrix points you to the right TaxAce agent.

Five Best Prompts for Webtrix

1. Start the guided process

Use this when you have an idea and want Webtrix to guide you.

Start

Webtrix begins planning by asking whether you have a plan, concept, or document, then asks only what it needs.

2. Build a landing page

Build a landing page for [TaxAce or client name]. The goal is [goal], and the main action visitors should take is [CTA and where it leads]. The offer is [offer]. It will be hosted on [GHL / GitHub / Vercel]. Here is what I already have: [copy, logo, colors, photos, reference sites, or "nothing yet"]. Ask me only what you cannot decide yourself.

3. Build a multi-page website

Build a multi-page website for [TaxAce or client name]. The goal is [goal], and the main action is [CTA]. I expect these pages: [page list, or "recommend the pages"]. It will be hosted on [GHL / GitHub / Vercel]. Here is what I already have: [copy, brand assets, references]. Use separate real pages, not one page with tabs.

4. Review my plan, then build from it

Attach your planning document, blueprint, or requirements.

Here is my plan for [project] for [TaxAce or client name]. Review it, tell me what you would keep, change, and add to make it convert better, and then ask me only for what is still missing before you build it.

5. Redesign or rewrite an existing page

Attach the page's HTML file, a saved copy, or screenshots. If you share a link, Webtrix will tell you if it cannot open it and will ask for the source instead.

Redesign this page for [TaxAce or client name]: [attach file or screenshots]. The goal is [goal] and the main action is [CTA]. Keep these parts exactly as they are: [approved copy, brand elements, facts]. Improve the hook, structure, design, and SEO.

After Delivery: Revisions

Revisions are simple. Open the files, then tell Webtrix what to change, for example:

  • "Make the hero headline more direct."
  • "Swap the second section's image for a darker mood."
  • "Add an FAQ question about [topic]."
  • "Change the button label and destination."

Webtrix edits the delivered files without restarting the process. If a change alters the strategy, such as a different goal, audience, or offer, it will say so and confirm the updated plan with you first.

Glossary

  • CTA: the call to action, meaning the button or step you want visitors to take.
  • CONFIG: the settings block at the top of each file where you update links, contact details, and image URLs.
  • SEO: search engine optimization, so the page can be found in search.
  • AEO: answer engine optimization, so the page gives clear, direct answers that search features can use.
  • GEO: generative engine optimization, so AI assistants can understand the page and quote it accurately.
  • JSON-LD: structured data in the page that tells search and AI systems what the page is about.

A Simple Rule of Thumb

Tell Webtrix the outcome you want and share what you already have. Say "yes" to its recommendations when they fit, and correct them when they do not. Approve the plan once, then review the finished files in your browser before you publish.
